# BITB-161: Arabic Morphology-Aware Topic Matching

**Status:** 🎯 Todo
**Renumbered:** 2026-09-21 from BITB-156 — ID collision with the Android session-limit story (#1083), which claimed BITB-156 first
**Priority:** P3 — recall improvement on an already-shipped, already-safe feature
**Size:** M (a clitic/affix-aware matcher plus re-validation is more than a keyword edit, less
than a full morphological analyzer)
**Created:** 2026-09-18
**Prompted by:** BITB-106, which denylisted four Arabic keywords (`حب`, `أمل`, `يأس`, `عفو`, `قلق`)
rather than fix the underlying matching mechanism

## User Story

**As** the maintainer, **I want** Arabic topic tagging to recover the recall BITB-106 traded away,
**so that** `love`/`hope`/`forgiveness`/`anxiety` tag as many genuinely-relevant Arabic verses as
the other six supported languages do, without reintroducing the over-firing BITB-106 found.

## Why This Exists

Arabic is the one language matched by bare substring (`SUBSTRING_MATCH_LANGUAGES` in
`api/chat/topic_tagging.py`), because its clitics (`و` "and", `ب` "with", `ل` "for", `ال` "the",
...) attach directly to the following word with no space — a `\w` word boundary would miss most
real occurrences. But a bare substring on a short root also matches *inside* an unrelated word,
which is exactly what BITB-106 measured on the real Smith & Van Dyke corpus (31,102 verses):

| keyword | topic | real match count | example unrelated words it also matched |
|---|---|---|---|
| `حب` (2 chars) | love | 1,071 verses (3.44%) | `صاحبه` "his companion", the proper names `رحبعام` (Rehoboam) / `حبرون` (Hebron), `فحبلت` "she conceived" |
| `أمل` | hope | 397 verses (1.28%) | `كامل` "complete", `حامل` "bearer/pregnant", `عامل` "worker", `الارامل` "the widows" |
| `يأس` | hope | 74 verses | `قياس`/`القياس` "measure", `رياسه` "rulership", the name `ابياساف` (Abiasaph) |
| `عفو` | forgiveness | 15 verses | verb forms of a different root (`يعفو`/`تعفو`/`اعفو`) |
| `قلق` | anxiety | 26 verses | `اللقلق` "the stork", verb forms of an unrelated root (`تتقلقل`/`يتقلقل`) |

BITB-106 denylisted all five rather than accept the noise — the safe, low-effort call for a story
already carrying five languages' worth of validation. This story is the deferred harder fix.

A clitic-anchored pattern (require the match to start the token, after stripping known
proclitics) was tried as a quick experiment during BITB-106 and **helps but does not solve it**:

| keyword | bare substring | clitic-anchored | still wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| `حب` | 1,071 | 793 | still matches `حبرون` (Hebron), `فحبلت` ("conceived") |
| `أمل` | 397 | 158 | still matches `كامل` ("complete") |
| `يأس` | 74 | 13 | — |

Proper nouns and words that happen to *start* with the same three letters (not just contain them)
are the residual — clitic-stripping alone can't distinguish "الحبرون" (a place name with the
definite article) from "الحب" (the word "love" with the definite article) by pattern shape alone.

## Proposed Approach (not decided — this is the open question the story is for)

Options, roughly in order of effort:

1. **A small denylist of whole-word exceptions** per keyword (e.g. never match when the matched
   span is immediately followed by the specific characters that spell `رعام`, `رون`, `مل`, ... —
   i.e., encode the *specific* false positives BITB-106 found as negative patterns). Cheap, but
   brittle: it fixes the corpus BITB-106 actually scanned, not the general case, and a new false
   positive in a different corpus/translation would need its own patch.
2. **A curated allowlist of Arabic word *forms*** per keyword (all attested inflections/clitic
   combinations of `حب`, `أمل`, etc., generated by hand or with a lemmatizer) instead of matching
   the bare root at all. More correct, more upfront work, and needs a native/expert reviewer to
   validate rather than the model-only checking BITB-106 could do.
3. **A real Arabic morphological analyzer** (e.g. CAMeL Tools, Farasa) to lemmatize each verse
   token and compare lemmas instead of surface substrings. Correct in general, but a real new
   dependency (and likely a real new service/model, not a pure-Python regex swap) for a feature
   that is currently a no-op behind `topic_boosting_enabled` — a heavy fix for a feature not yet
   proven to help (see BITB-116, gating the flag itself on real A/B numbers).
4. **Do nothing further** — the denylist BITB-106 shipped is safe and correct as far as it goes;
   this story only exists to recover recall, not to fix a correctness bug. Legitimate outcome if
   BITB-116 finds topic boosting doesn't move the numbers enough to justify the investment.
